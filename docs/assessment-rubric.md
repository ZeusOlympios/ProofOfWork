# Assessment Rubric

## Purpose

Provide reviewers with a consistent way to evaluate project evidence. The rubric is a proposed design and has not yet been validated.

## Scoring scale

- 0 — No assessable evidence provided.
- 1 — Limited: substantial gaps or incorrect behaviour.
- 2 — Developing: partially effective, with notable gaps.
- 3 — Competent: meets the criterion with minor weaknesses.
- 4 — Strong: consistent, convincing evidence against the criterion.

A score of zero means evidence was not demonstrated in the assessment; it does not establish that the candidate lacks the underlying skill.

## Weighted criteria

| Criterion | Weight | Evidence to examine |
|---|---:|---|
| Code quality | 30 | Readability, structure, maintainability and design choices |
| Problem-solving | 30 | Requirements, correctness, edge cases and trade-offs |
| Testing | 20 | Relevant tests, expected behaviour and failure handling |
| Documentation | 20 | Setup instructions, explanation, design decisions and limitations |
| Total | 100 | |

## Calculation

For each category:

Weighted points = (rating / 4) × category weight.

Total score = sum of all four weighted category scores.

Example: Code quality rating 3/4 contributes (3 / 4) × 30 = 22.5 points.

The theoretical maximum is 100.

## Required evidence record

For each category, the reviewer should record:
- Rating from 0 to 4.
- Specific repository file, test, behaviour or observation supporting the rating.
- Short explanation of why the evidence matches the rating.
- Missing evidence or uncertainty.
- Reviewer identity and assessment date, stored privately.

## Practical task

Use a short task with the same core requirements and comparable difficulty for candidates applying to the same role. Record completion, reasoning and observed behaviour. Allow appropriate accommodations.

A task should assess relevant skills rather than speed alone. Do not compare results from materially different tasks as though they were equivalent.

## Important limitations

The score is a structured signal, not a guarantee of job performance. Reviewers may disagree, repositories may contain copied work, and candidates may have unequal resources. Scores should not automatically reject candidates without an appropriate review process.
